Limits The Other Side Enforces

Biscuit tokens

Issued by the retailer’s authority, bound to the store’s DID, with a spend cap and the categories it may buy. A holder can add checks, never remove them.

A company verifies the token offline with the authority’s public key: no call back at the moment of the deal.

A signed quote chain

Request, quote, acceptance, confirmation: each signed, each naming the previous by digest.

An audit needs only the DID documents and the authority’s key, even after the token has expired.

Why not an API key. A plain bearer secret cannot be narrowed by its holder, cannot be checked by a company without asking the issuer, and does not show who signed a commitment. Cost: a higher cap means a new token from the authority.

Biscuit tokens are verified with the authority’s public key and carry Datalog checks; attenuation adds checks and can never widen. The spending grant names a cap, a currency, the categories and the site, and the token is bound to the store’s DID. The quote messages form a chain by SHA-256 digest, and an offline audit verifies every signature, the chain and the token as it stood at the time of the commitment.
